Here are my homemade switches,

This project involved creating a paper prototype, an aligator clip prototype, and then a final sewn implementation of two homemade switches that control an LED. The first switch I created was the two satin-stitched patches of conductive thread; this one was on my negative side of the battery. The second switch I made was using conductive fabric. I liked this one because it can be used like a sustain switch and a momentary switch because the conductive fabric holds itself fairly strongly when pushed down to the other connection. I had a bit of difficulty because even when my satin-stitched patches weren't connected, the LED would light up when the fabric switch was on. I used an aligator clip and another LED to trace where the short to negative was, and it was between the satin-stitched patches. I inspected thoroughly, and I hypothesize that there was a bit of moisture on the fabric, causing the short, because there is NO overlap or loose threads connecting the patches.    

A tip for myself would be to hide my traces with the running stitch on the perimeter. I did one of them in the spaces between the  running stitches--completing the circle, which didn't look as good as the other way, where I ran each stitch right next to the running stitch, which hid them much better.
